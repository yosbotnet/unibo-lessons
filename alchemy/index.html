What this is

Training a language model involves dozens of choices nobody can derive from first principles: the learning rate, the batch size, how long to warm up, how much weight decay, which optimizer, how to scale any of it to a bigger model. Practitioners mostly know these things the way an alchemist knew a recipe. Stanford’s CS 312, Deep Learning Alchemy (Tatsunori Hashimoto and Suhas Kotha) takes a stricter line: you understand a training phenomenon when you can predict the outcome of an experiment before running it. Its grade is 85% prediction quizzes about one small reference model. This companion follows that course unit by unit, in its own words, with interactive figures, sealed predictions you lock before reading the answer, and a ledger that measures how well calibrated you are.

It has one extra purpose the original does not. If you direct an agent (a person or an LLM) that runs training experiments for you, the hard part is not running them; it is knowing which conclusions to trust. Every unit ends with audit drills: short, fictional research logs with a conclusion in them, and the question “what would you challenge before accepting this?”

How to use it. Read Unit 0 once. Then, for each unit: lock every prediction before you scroll past it, do the drills, and check your ledger at the end. The ledger lives in your browser only; export it if you switch devices.
